## The Trikon Parikrama - Why You Need a Car Here

Vindhyachal is not a single temple but a **sacred triangle of three hilltop shrines**:

1. **Vindhyavasini Devi** - the main Shakti Peeth in the town centre, busy from dawn.
2. **Ashtabhuja Devi** - the eight-armed Goddess on a hill, reached by steps.
3. **Kali Khoh** - the fierce form of Kali in a cave-like shrine.

Completing the **Trikon Parikrama** (circumambulating all three) on foot takes most of a day and involves several kilometres of hill road between shrines. A taxi waiting at each point turns an exhausting trek into a comfortable 3-4 hour darshan, which is exactly why most families book a car rather than rely on shared autos.

## Best Route & Timing

**Varanasi → NH19 → Mirzapur → Vindhyachal**

The road runs alongside the Ganga for stretches and is smooth four-lane almost the whole way. Leave by **6:00 AM** to reach for the morning aarti and beat the heat on the hill shrines. **Navratri** (twice a year, March-April and September-October) is peak season - the town is packed and an early start is essential.

## Planning a Comfortable Trip

A little planning makes the Vindhyavasini darshan smooth for everyone:

- **Families and elders:** the main Vindhyavasini temple is easy to reach, but **Ashtabhuja Devi and Kali Khoh sit on the hill and involve steps and short climbs**. For senior or limited-mobility pilgrims we drop as close as the police barricades allow, plan rest stops, and keep the pace gentle - a wheelchair is difficult on the steps, so allow extra time. Keep children close on the busy hill paths.
- **During Navratri:** the town is intensely crowded and the hill gets hot - start at dawn, carry water, and wear comfortable shoes.
