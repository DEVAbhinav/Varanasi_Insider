## Day 1 · Arrival, Temple Circuit & Twilight Aarti

### Arrival & Check-in (12:00 – 13:30)

Fly into Lal Bahadur Shastri Airport (VNS) or arrive by rail at Varanasi Junction / Cantt. Chauffeured pickup in a sanitized AC vehicle, hotel check-in, briefing over welcome drink, and a quick lunch stop.[^arrival]

**Flight timing note:** Ideal arrival is by noon. If your flight lands after 3 PM, we compress the temple circuit to the 2–3 essential stops (Kaal Bhairav + Kashi Vishwanath + Annapurna) and prioritize the sunset boat ride. Remaining temples shift to Day 2 morning.

### Afternoon Temple Circuit (13:30 – 16:45)

#### Kaal Bhairav Temple (13:30 – 14:15)

![Kaal Bhairav Temple — the divine guardian of Kashi](https://res.cloudinary.com/dkntlqbwr/image/upload/kashitaxi/kashitaxi/varanasi-kal-bhairav-p.jpeg)
*Kaal Bhairav — the Kotwal (divine police chief) of Kashi. Traditional belief says you must seek his permission before exploring the city.*

We start here, following the pilgrimage sequence locals have followed for centuries. Kaal Bhairav is the guardian deity of Kashi — the tradition says no journey into the city's sacred geography begins without his permission.[^kaal]

**Unique tradition:** This is one of the rare Hindu temples where **alcohol is offered** as part of the ritual — madira (liquor) offering is the traditional practice. Your guide handles the cultural context.

**Entry:** Free. **Hours:** 5:00 AM – 10:00 PM. **Time needed:** 30–40 minutes. Queue is typically short.

#### Kashi Vishwanath Complex (14:30 – 16:00)

![Kashi Vishwanath — the Ganga Dwar entrance to the spiritual axis of Varanasi](https://res.cloudinary.com/dkntlqbwr/image/upload/kashitaxi/kashitaxi/kashi-vishwanath-ganga-dwar.jpeg)
*The Ganga Dwar entrance to Kashi Vishwanath — where the ₹339-crore corridor meets the 1780 temple.*

The spiritual axis of Varanasi. With 3 days, there's no rush — you get the full complex, not just the Jyotirlinga darshan. The circuit covers three temples walkable within the KV Corridor:[^kv]

1. **Kashi Vishwanath Temple** — One of twelve Jyotirlingas. Rebuilt 1780 by Maharani Ahilyabai Holkar. Daily footfall: 10,000–15,000 pilgrims.
2. **Annapurna Temple** — Goddess of nourishment, famous for the annadan (community kitchen) tradition.
3. **Vishalakshi Temple** — One of 51 Shakti Peethas. Adjacent to KV, easy to include in the same visit.

**Practical notes:**
- **Sugam Darshan (fast-track):** ₹300. Reduces queue from 20–120 min to 10–15 min. Book at shrikashivishwanath.org or same-day at the helpdesk.
- **Security:** Mobile phones, leather items, and cameras prohibited inside. Lockers ₹20–50 at nearby shops. Enter through **Gate 4** for shortest queue.
- **Queue reality:** Weekday afternoons (Mon–Thu): 20–40 min. Weekends: 60–120 min. Festival days: 2–10+ hours.
- **KV Corridor accessibility:** The 2021 corridor has accessible pathways suitable for elderly visitors and wheelchairs.

#### Old City Heritage Walk (16:00 – 16:45)

A 45-minute stroll from KV Complex through the living lanes of Varanasi:

- **Vishwanath Gali** — Silk shops, religious item stalls, flower garland vendors threading jasmine and marigold.
- **Thatheri Bazaar** — Brassware artisans hammering idols and utensils in workshops that have been here for generations.
- **Silk weaving peek** — Ducking into a loom workshop for a first taste of the weaving world (the deep atelier visit is on Day 3).
- **Quick food stop** — Kachori-sabzi at Ram Bhandar (₹25–35/plate) or a lassi at Blue Lassi Shop (₹60–120) if hunger strikes.[^oldcity][^food]

### Day 2 Temple & Campus Loop (09:30 – 12:30)

With the KV Complex done on Day 1, Day 2 morning covers the temple circuit's second wave:[^temples]

#### Sankat Mochan Hanuman Temple (09:30 – 10:15)

Founded by Tulsidas (the poet who wrote the Ramcharitmanas). This is where Varanasi's devout come for travel blessings and to resolve anxieties. The famed besan laddus are legendary — buy a box as prasad. Monkeys are residents; our guide manages the dynamic.

**Entry:** Free. **Time needed:** 30–40 minutes.

#### Durga Kund & Tulsi Manas Temple (10:15 – 11:00)

Two temples, side by side, with different energies:

- **Durga Kund (Monkey Temple):** Red sandstone, sacred pond, Shakti traditions. Entry free, open 5:00 AM – 10:00 PM. Watch for aggressive monkeys — bags zipped, food hidden.
- **Tulsi Manas Temple:** Where Tulsidas wrote the Ramcharitmanas. The walls are inscribed with verses from the text — the actual poetry carved into marble. Open 5:30 AM – 12:00 PM, 3:30 PM – 9:00 PM (midday closure). Free entry.

#### Banaras Hindu University & New Vishwanath Temple (11:00 – 12:00)

Asia's largest residential university. After the intensity of the old city, BHU's tree-lined drives and manicured lawns feel like a different planet.[^bhu]

- **New Vishwanath Temple (Birla Temple):** Free entry. Open 4:00 AM – 12:00 PM, 1:00 PM – 9:00 PM. Marble architecture — the antidote to old-city temple crowds.
- **Bharat Kala Bhavan Museum:** Miniature paintings, ancient sculptures, textile collections. One of India's finest university museums. **Hours:** 10:30 AM – 4:30 PM. **Closed:** Sundays + university holidays. **Entry:** ₹20 (Indians), ₹500 (foreigners).
- **Campus drive:** 15 peaceful minutes through tree-lined avenues before heading to Sarnath.

### Sarnath Deep Dive (12:30 – 16:00)

![Dhamek Stupa at Sarnath — the traditional site of Buddha's first sermon](https://res.cloudinary.com/dkntlqbwr/image/upload/kashitaxi/kashitaxi/Sarnath-Stoopa-Square-beautiful-large.jpeg)
*The Dhamek Stupa (43.6m high) — marking the traditional site where the Buddha, already enlightened at Bodh Gaya, delivered his first sermon.*

With 3.5 hours (vs 90 minutes on a same-day tour), you get the most thorough Sarnath experience possible. The 10–13 km drive from BHU takes 25–35 minutes.[^sarnath]

**What you'll cover:**

1. **Dhamek Stupa** — 43.6m cylindrical tower with Gupta-period stone carvings. The traditional site of the first sermon (Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta).
2. **Chaukhandi Stupa** — Where Buddha first met his five disciples post-enlightenment.
3. **Ashoka Pillar** — Original shaft in situ. The Lion Capital (inside the museum) became India's National Emblem.
4. **Archaeological Museum** — India's first site museum (est. 1910). 6,832 artifacts. Crown jewel: Lion Capital + Dharmachakra wheel (24 spokes → India's flag).
5. **Mulagandha Kuti Vihar** — Modern temple (1931) with Japanese frescoes by Kosetsu Nosu. Sacred Bodhi tree from the Anuradhapura lineage.
6. **International monastery cluster** — Thai, Tibetan, Cambodian, Sri Lankan temples (free, open daily). With 3 days, you have time to actually step inside 2–3 monasteries rather than drive past.
7. **80-ft Buddha statue** — The seated bronze Buddha near the monasteries.

### Cultural Evening (18:00 – 20:00)

This is the 3-day exclusive — the experience that makes the extra night worth it.

**Option A — Private Classical Music Recital (60–90 min, ₹2,000–5,000)**

An intimate session in a musician's home, heritage haveli, or hotel courtyard. Varanasi is the historical seat of the **Banaras Gharana** — one of Hindustani classical music's most important lineages. Expect sitar or sarod (melody) + tabla (rhythm) + tanpura (drone), moving through alap (slow exposition) → jod (rhythmic development) → gat (full composition). Evening ragas like Yaman or Desh, with the artist explaining the emotional structure to listeners. Groups of 5–20 maximum — far more personal than a concert hall.[^culture]

**Option B — Kathak Performance (60 min, ₹2,000–5,000)**

Solo or duet Kathak in a cultural centre or heritage space. Rhythmic footwork (tatkar), spins (chakkar), and expressive storytelling (abhinaya) depicting episodes from Krishna's life or Radha-Krishna love poetry. Accompanied by tabla, harmonium, and vocalist. Both the Lucknow and Banaras Gharanas of Kathak have strong local practitioners.

**Option C — Guided Deep Food Walk (90 min, ₹1,200–2,000)**

A structured walk through Godowlia, Chowk, and ghat-side lanes covering 6–8 stops beyond what Day 1's post-aarti walk covered: street chaat stalls, sweet shops, thandai variations, and seasonal specialties.

**Dinner & overnight** at hotel.

## Day 3 · Ramnagar Fort, Silk Ateliers, Markets & Farewell

### Extended Morning Spiritual Circuit (07:00 – 09:30)

With 3 days, you can complete the deeper temple circuit that pilgrims and Kashi residents consider the full theological loop:

#### Ghat-Side Temples (07:00 – 08:30)

These sit along the riverfront, walkable in sequence:

- **Kedareshwar Temple (Kedar Ghat)** — Part of the Panch Tirtha pilgrimage circuit. This temple connects Varanasi to the Kedarnath tradition — pilgrims who can't travel to the Himalayas complete their Kedar darshan here. Free entry.
- **Mankameshwar Temple (near Lalita Ghat)** — "Wish-fulfilling" Shiva temple. Local families visit before major life decisions. Free entry.
- **Sakshi Vinayak (Scindia Ghat)** — "Witness Ganesha," considered the divine witness to all events in Kashi. Small but significant. Free entry.
- **Ratneshwar Mahadev (near Scindia Ghat)** — The famous "leaning temple" partially submerged in the Ganges. Lean varies by river level — monsoon shows maximum tilt. Best photographed from the water or adjacent ghat.

#### Tridev Temple (08:30 – 09:00)

Houses Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva in one temple — rare because standalone Brahma temples are extremely uncommon in Hinduism. Visiting Tridev completes the theological trinity circuit that the core darshan loop (Shaiva at KV + Shakti at Vishalakshi) doesn't cover.

**Entry:** Free. **Hours:** 5:00 AM – 12:00 PM, 3:00 PM – 9:00 PM. **Time needed:** 20–30 minutes.

**Breakfast** (09:00 – 09:30) at hotel or morning food stall.

### Ramnagar Fort & Museum (10:00 – 12:00)

The 1750 Chunar sandstone palace of the Kashi Naresh (Maharaja of Benares) sits on the eastern bank of the Ganges, opposite Tulsi Ghat. Built by Maharaja Balwant Singh, it's Mughal-influenced architecture with carved balconies, open courtyards, and two distinctive white towers. The current Maharaja (Anant Narayan Singh) still resides here — when the flag flies, he's in residence.

**Getting there:** 14 km from main ghats, 30–40 min by road. Fastest route: Ramnagar Bridge from BHU side (~10 min from campus).

**What's inside the Saraswati Bhawan Museum:**

- **Vintage American cars** — A rare automobile collection housed in the fort courtyard.
- **Royal armoury** — Swords, ornate matchlocks, daggers from Africa, Burma, Japan, and hookahs. The weapons gallery alone takes 20 minutes.
- **Astronomical clock (1852)** — Made by the court astronomer. Shows time, year, month, week, day, and positions of the Sun, Moon, and planets. One of India's most unusual horological artifacts.
- **Durbar Hall** — Original frescoes, royal reception rooms, portraits of the Maharaja lineage.
- **Medieval costumes** — Including kimkhwa silk (the finest Varanasi weaver product), bejeweled palanquins in lotus-flower shape, and silver elephant saddles.
- **Manuscripts** — Religious texts and 535 Mughal miniature-style illustrations with ornate floral borders.

**The Ramlila tradition (UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage, 2008):**
This is the world's longest continuous Ramayana performance — 31 days (vs the typical 10-day abridged version elsewhere). Started c. 1830 under Maharaja Udit Narayan Singh, the entire area becomes an open-air theatre with audiences walking alongside performers through permanent stages (Ashoka Vatika, Janakpuri, Lanka). The Maharaja rides a decorated elephant at the head of the procession. Over a million pilgrims attend across the season (September–October). Some roles have been inherited by the same families since 1835.

**Hours:** 10:00 AM – 4:30 PM (winter), 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM (summer). **Closed during Dussehra** (the Ramlila is hosted here).
**Entry:** ₹15–75 (Indians), ₹150–200 (foreigners).

**Accessibility note:** The fort has steps and uneven surfaces — not wheelchair-friendly. Ground-level courtyards are manageable but the white towers require climbing. For elderly travellers who find it difficult, we substitute a relaxed ghat-side morning or car-side photo stop on the bridge with fort views.[^ramnagar]

### Silk Weaving Atelier — Deep Visit (12:30 – 14:00)

This isn't the 15-minute peek from Day 1's heritage walk. This is a 90-minute guided session in a dedicated weaver's workshop in one of Varanasi's master weaving clusters (Sarai Mohana, Madanpura, or Kotwa).[^silk]

**What unfolds:**

1. **Warping demonstration** — Setting up the parallel warp threads on the loom. A single saree's warp setup takes a full day.
2. **Jacquard mechanism** — See how the punched cards (traditional) or digital input controls which threads lift for each weft pass, creating intricate patterns.
3. **Zari work close-up** — Real gold/silver-wrapped thread woven as supplementary weft. Learn the burn test (real gold melts safely; cheap imitation burns) and weight test (quality zari adds 200–400g per saree).
4. **Kadhua technique** — The most labour-intensive method, where each motif is woven individually and threads cut behind. A single kadhua saree can take 3–6 months.
5. **Conversation with master weaver** — Understand design lineage, family traditions, market pressures, and the tension between handloom heritage and powerloom economics.

Yes — the 1750 palace of the Maharaja of Benares houses rare American vintage cars, an 1852 astronomical clock, royal armoury, medieval costumes, and riverside Ganges views.
