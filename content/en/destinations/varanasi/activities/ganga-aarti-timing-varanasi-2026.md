# Ganga Aarti Timing in Varanasi: Assi vs Dashashwamedh

> **This is a venue-comparison page, not a live timetable.** Use the [Assi Ghat timing guide](/en/assi-ghat-aarti-timings-2026) for Subah-e-Banaras and Assi evening timings. Use the [Dashashwamedh Ghat guide](/en/dashashwamedh-ghat-ganga-aarti-timing) for the main evening ceremony. Verify locally on the day.

Varanasi does not have one interchangeable “Ganga Aarti.” The morning programme at Assi and the evening ceremony at Dashashwamedh serve different visitors and feel very different on the ground.

For initial planning, Assi morning is generally around **5:00 AM in summer and 5:30 AM in winter**; Assi evening is around **6:30 PM in summer and 6:00 PM in winter**. Dashashwamedh evening takes place around sunset and is commonly planned around **6:00–7:00 PM**, depending on season. These are seasonal windows, not a live guarantee.

## Quick Comparison

| Decision | Assi morning | Assi evening | Dashashwamedh evening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Character | Worship, devotional music, yoga and dawn | Smaller neighbourhood evening ritual | Large, highly choreographed multi-priest ceremony |
| Best for | Early risers, photographers, quieter spiritual atmosphere | Visitors staying in south Varanasi who prefer easier access | First-time visitors seeking the iconic spectacle |
| Crowd | Usually the easiest of the three | Usually more manageable than Dashashwamedh | Densest, especially weekends and festivals |
| Arrival buffer | 30–45 minutes | 30–45 minutes | At least 45–60 minutes; more on peak dates |
| Best view | Steps for the programme; boat only if combining a sunrise loop | Steps or river, subject to boat operations | Steps for atmosphere; river for a wider view |

## Choose Assi Morning for Subah-e-Banaras

Subah-e-Banaras is more than a short lamp ritual. It combines Vedic recitation, devotional music and public yoga as the riverfront moves from darkness into daylight.

Choose it if you:

- prefer a contemplative start rather than a large evening crowd
- are staying around Assi, Bhadaini, Lanka or BHU
- want to pair the programme with a sunrise walk or boat ride
- can manage a pre-dawn start

The steps usually give a richer view of the full programme than a boat. A boat makes sense when the Aarti is one part of a longer sunrise route.

## Choose Dashashwamedh Evening for the Iconic Ceremony

Dashashwamedh is the ceremony most first-time visitors picture: multiple priests, large lamps, amplified chanting and a dense audience on the steps and river.

Choose it if you:

- want the city's best-known evening ritual
- are comfortable with crowds and old-city walking
- can arrive early and avoid a tight train, flight or temple connection afterward
- want to watch from a boat opposite the platforms, subject to river permissions

There is no official ticket required to stand on the public ghat steps. Treat claims of guaranteed “VIP” access cautiously and understand exactly what a paid seat or boat booking includes.

## Choose Assi Evening for Easier Access

Assi's evening ceremony is smaller than Dashashwamedh's. It can suit families, older travellers or visitors based in south Varanasi who value simpler road access and a less compressed crowd.

It is not a substitute for Dashashwamedh if your priority is scale. It is a distinct neighbourhood ceremony.

## Steps or Boat?

Choose the **steps** for sound, ritual detail and participation in the crowd. Choose a **boat** for a wider view and easier photography, but only when authorities permit river operations.

During high water, strong current or official restrictions, boat services may be suspended. A booking should never override a police or river-safety instruction. See the [evening Aarti boat guide](/en/evening-boat-ride-varanasi-ganga-aarti) for the on-water experience rather than using this comparison page as a price list.

## Mobility and Exit Planning

Dashashwamedh often requires a longer walk through pedestrian controls and crowded lanes. Assi generally has easier road access near the upper ghat, although the steps can still be slippery or uneven.

For anyone with limited mobility:

- confirm the closest permitted drop point on the day
- avoid depending on a last-minute auto in the exit crowd
- carry only essentials
- choose upper steps rather than pushing toward the front
- allow the first crowd wave to leave before moving

## Monsoon and Festival Reality

Aarti normally continues in some form, but the platform, audience area, duration and boat access can change. Dev Deepawali, Kartik Purnima and other major dates need a separate crowd plan; ordinary seasonal timings should not be applied to them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Ganga Aarti is best for a first visit?

Dashashwamedh evening Aarti is the iconic choice. Assi morning is better for visitors who value a calmer dawn atmosphere over scale.

### Is Assi Aarti the same as Dashashwamedh Aarti?

No. Assi's morning programme includes devotional music and yoga, while Dashashwamedh is the large evening ceremony. Assi also hosts a smaller evening Aarti.

### Which is easier for families or older travellers?

Assi is often easier to approach and navigate. Dashashwamedh may still be suitable with an early arrival, an agreed drop point and a patient exit plan.
