# Varanasi in January 2026: Weather, Packing & Fog Survival Guide

## Varanasi in January 2026: A Local’s Guide to Surviving the Chill & Fog
Is Varanasi cold in January? Yes. In fact, many tourists arrive unprepared for just how bone-chilling it gets.

While the afternoons are sunny and perfect for walking (22°C), the nights and early mornings drop to a shivering 9°C (48°F). But the real challenge isn’t just the cold—it’s the North Indian Fog that disrupts flights and trains.

I’ve lived through countless winters here, and this guide will help you pack right, avoid travel delays, and actually enjoy the misty magic of Kashi.

## 1. The Weather Reality Check (January 2026)
Average High: 22°C (Pleasant sunny days)

Average Low: 8°C - 9°C (Very cold nights/mornings)

Sunrise: ~6:45 AM (Often hidden by fog until 9:00 AM)

Sunset: ~5:30 PM (Darkness falls early!)

### The "Barefoot" Challenge
Here is the one thing most blogs won't tell you: The stone floors of temples are freezing. Since you must take off your shoes at Kashi Vishwanath and other temples, your feet will go numb within minutes.

Insider Tip: Pack thick woolen socks. You are allowed to wear socks inside the temple, and they are a lifesaver.

## 2. The Fog Survival Guide: How to Avoid Getting Stuck
January brings dense fog across the entire Indo-Gangetic plain. This peaks between January 1st and January 15th, often reducing visibility to zero in the mornings.

### Flight Delays are Real
If your flight is scheduled to land before 11:00 AM, there is a 60% chance it will be delayed or diverted to Lucknow/Delhi.

My Advice: If possible, book a flight landing after 12:00 PM. The sun usually burns off the fog by noon.

Trains from Delhi (like the Vande Bharat or Shiv Ganga Express) are often delayed by 6 to 10 hours in January due to fog.

## 3. What to Pack for Varanasi in January
Don't be fooled by photos of sunny ghats. Here is your essential packing list:

Layers, Layers, Layers: You’ll need a heavy jacket at 7 AM, but by 1 PM, you’ll be sweating in a t-shirt.

Moisturizer & Lip Balm: The winter air here is extremely dry.

N95 Mask: On non-windy days, the air quality (AQI) can dip. A mask is good for sensitive travelers.

Power Bank: Cold batteries drain faster, and you'll be taking hundreds of photos.

Ear Muffs / Cap: Especially for the morning boat ride—the wind on the river cuts through you.

## 4. Safety for Seniors & Families
Traveling with elderly parents?

Slippery Ghats: The morning dew + fog makes the stone steps slippery. Avoid the steep stairs at the smaller ghats. Stick to Dashashwamedh or Assi Ghat which have wider, cleaner steps.

Walking Limits: The Old City lanes are long and confusing. For seniors, we highly recommend hiring a Tempo Traveller so they have a comfortable seat waiting for them between temple visits.

## FAQ: Quick Answers
Q: Can I take a holy dip in the Ganga in January?

A: Yes, but the water is around 15°C. I recommend taking the dip around 12:00 PM when the sun is out, rather than at sunrise.

Q: Is the Ganga Aarti time different in winter?

A: Yes! Because it gets dark early, the Aarti starts around 6:00 PM (instead of 7 PM in summer). Reach by 5:00 PM to get a good seat.
