## The truth about Manikarnika: what to expect
- Cremations run 24/7; multiple pyres may burn at once.
- Bodies arrive on bamboo stretchers; families chant prayers.
- Smoke, ash, and sandalwood scent are part of the ritual.
- No shops or tourist setup; this is a sacred space.

## How to visit respectfully
- No photography. Phones and cameras away.
- Dress modestly; cover shoulders and legs.
- Walk quietly, stand to the side, avoid blocking paths.
- Plan 30-45 minutes for observation; your driver waits at Maidagin.

## Avoiding mistakes at Manikarnika
- **Direct drop scam:** No car can go beyond Maidagin. Anyone claiming a hidden route is misleading you.
- **Photo penalties:** Locals respond strongly to photography. Do not attempt it.
- **Disrespectful behavior:** Loud talk, revealing clothes, or laughter can prompt locals to ask you to leave.

## Manikarnika FAQ

### Is it okay for foreigners to visit?
Yes, if you are quiet, modestly dressed, and do not photograph. Respect is expected.

### Can you drop closer than Maidagin?
No. Maidagin is the legal drop; the final 200 m is on foot.

### Can I bring children?
Teens are fine. Under-10s are not advised due to the intensity of cremations.

### Is there a guide to explain the cremation process?
There are no official guides; some workers may explain for a small tip. We can connect you if requested.

### Can I combine Manikarnika with evening Ganga Aarti?
Yes. Common plan: Manikarnika at 4 PM, then Dashashwamedh Aarti. We offer a 3-hour evening circuit with waiting.
