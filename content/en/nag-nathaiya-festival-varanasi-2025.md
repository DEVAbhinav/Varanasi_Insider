Micro-tip: carry a 70-200mm lens and polarized filter to nail the serpent stance without flare.

*Last Updated: October 25, 2025*

**Quick Answer:** Nag Nathaiya (locally called *Lakkha Mela* due to the huge crowd) is the dramatic reenactment of **Lord Krishna subduing the serpent Kaliya**. In 2025 it is **confirmed for Saturday, 25 October (Kartik Shukla Chaturthi)** at **Tulsi Ghat (near Assi)**. The boy portraying Krishna dives from a symbolic Kadamba tree into the Ganga, emerges standing on a multi‑hooded serpent effigy, and plays the flute—an unforgettable spiritual tableau.

## 🐍 What is Nag Nathaiya (Lakkha Mela)?
Nag Nathaiya celebrates Krishna's **victory over Kaliya Nāga**—symbolizing conquering **poison, pride, and chaos**. The staging at Tulsi Ghat links Vaishnava devotion, local theater, and the perennial sacred presence of the Ganga. The name *Lakkha Mela* reflects the traditional belief that **a lakh (100k+) devotees** turn up.

**Why Tulsi Ghat?** It was associated with **Goswami Tulsidas**, anchoring the performance in devotional literary heritage.

```
3️⃣ Kadamba Tree Climb: Temporary tree scaffold near river edge
4️⃣ Sacred Dive: Krishna leaps into Ganga (symbolizing entry into Yamuna mythically)
5️⃣ Serpent Effigy Encounter: Multi‑hood Kaliya floats mid‑stream
6️⃣ Emergence Pose: Krishna stands on hoods → Iconic photo moment
7️⃣ Flute & Aarti: Priests, conch, cymbals, mass chanting "Haré Krishna"
8️⃣ Benediction & dispersal (sun lowering)
```
**Photo Peak:** 30–60 seconds of Krishna stabilizing upright on the serpent—boats & ghat roar with chants.
