# Sorahia Mela Varanasi 2026: Lakshmi Kund Mahalakshmi Vrat, Jitiya Snan & Ground Logistics

> **Verified 2026 Festival Schedule:** Sorahia Mela runs for 16 consecutive days, starting on **Saturday, 19 September 2026** (Radha Ashtami / Bhadrapada Shukla Ashtami) and culminating on **Sunday, 4 October 2026** (Ashwin Krishna Ashtami / Jivitputrika Vrat & Maha Udyapan).

Every autumn, tucked just behind the crowded arteries connecting Godowlia to Sigra, Varanasi hosts one of its most authentic, intensely devotional folk pilgrimages: the **Sorahia Mela** (सोरहिया मेला) at **Lakshmi Kund**.

While riverfront spectacles like Dev Deepawali and Ganga Aarti attract international tourists, Sorahia Mela belongs to traditional mothers, families, and pilgrims across Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and Jharkhand. For 16 continuous days, hundreds of thousands of women gather at Lakshmi Kund to observe the sacred **Mahalakshmi Vrat**, seeking the blessings of wealth, family lineage protection, and the long life of their children.

On the 16th day—which coincides with the sacred **Jivitputrika (Jitiya) Vrat**—the festival reaches an electric crescendo. Over 100,000 devotees descend upon the stone embankments before sunrise, carrying painted bamboo baskets (*pauti*), lit earthen lamps, and freshly fried *thekua* and *pirukiya*.

However, for outstation families arriving in Varanasi, navigating Luxa during the fair can be baffling. Strict police diversions close Luxa Road to four-wheelers, slippery stone steps require caution, and aggressive street vendors inflate ritual samagri prices.

This guide provides the unvarnished local reality: verified dates, ritual rules, pedestrian shortcuts, elderly safety protocols, honest pricing, and pre-arranged festival transport.

## Mythological Significance: Lakshmi Kund & Sorahia Mahadev

Lakshmi Kund is not a modern municipal pond; it is an ancient tirtha deeply rooted in Kashi's sacred geography.

### 1. Scriptural Roots (*Kashi Khand*, *Skanda Purana*)
Chapter 70 of the *Kashi Khand* mentions Lakshmi Kund as a premier site of spiritual absolution. Bathing here during Bhadrapada is believed to destroy severe financial distress (*Daridrya*) and bless childless households with noble progeny.

According to local Sthala Purana, during the Treta Yuga, Lord Rama, Mata Sita, and Lakshmana created three holy water bodies in Kashi: Ram Kund, Sita Kund, and **Lakshmana Kund**. Over millennia, Lakshmana Kund became the primary seat for worshipping Goddess Lakshmi and evolved into modern **Lakshmi Kund**.

### 2. The Unique Holy Trinity Sanctum
The temple overlooking the eastern bank of the kund is one of the very few shrines in India housing a unified Trinity:
- **Maa Mahalakshmi** (Center)
- **Maa Mahasaraswati** (Flanking)
- **Maa Mahakali** (Flanking)

The sanctum is guarded by **Mayuri Yogini** (one of the 64 Chausatthi Yoginis dispatched by Shiva to protect the borders of Varanasi) and **Shikhi Chandi Devi**.

### 3. Sorahia Mahadev (Mahalakshmeeshwar Lingam)
Directly adjacent to the kund steps stands an ancient stone Shiva Lingam. In scriptural records, it is called **Mahalakshmeeshwar Mahadev**—consecrated by Goddess Lakshmi herself after severe penance. In local Banarasi parlance, because it is worshipped with 16 offerings throughout these 16 days, it is affectionately known across the city as **Sorahia Mahadev** (सोरहिया महादेव). Pilgrims consider the snan incomplete without offering bilva leaves and Gangajal to Sorahia Mahadev.

## The Ritual Architecture: The Sacred Rule of "16"

Throughout the 16 days, the number 16 governs every devotional act:

```
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │      THE SACRED RULE OF "16"        │
                  └──────────────────┬──────────────────┘
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                           ▼                           ▼
 🧵 Sorahia Dora            🪔 Solah Batti Deepak       🧺 16 Shodashopachara
 16 knots tied on raw       Earthen lamp with 16 wicks   16 flowers, 16 dūrvā grass,
 cotton dipped in haldi     soaked in pure desi ghee     16 cloves, 16 cardamoms &
 worn around wrist/neck     lit at sanctum door          16 grains of unbroken rice
```

1. **The 16-Knot Sacred Thread (*Sorahia Dora*):**
   - Prepared by dipping raw unbleached cotton threads into fresh turmeric paste and saffron water.
   - 16 distinct knots are tied, each invoked with a name of Lakshmi's 16 manifestations (*Dhanya Lakshmi, Dhana Lakshmi, Santana Lakshmi, Vijaya Lakshmi*, etc.).
   - Women tie this on their right wrist (or wear it as a consecrated necklace) on Day 1. It remains untouched until the Day 16 Udyapan.
2. **The 16-Wick Earthen Lamp (*Solah Batti ka Deepak*):**
   - Devotees craft or purchase wide terracotta deepaks holding 16 hand-rolled cotton wicks.
   - Lit with cow ghee or sesame oil during evening aarti and placed carefully along the stone steps overlooking the water.
3. **The 16 Offerings (*Shodashopachara*):**
   - Every morning after the bath, pilgrims offer 16 blades of green dūrvā grass, 16 unbroken grains of rice (*akshat*), 16 betel leaves (*paan*), 16 supari, and 16 pieces of seasonal fruits.

## Day 16 Climax: Jivitputrika (Jitiya) Vrat & Pauti Traditions

The 16th day transforms the quiet neighborhood of Luxa into an ocean of devotees.

### What is Jivitputrika (Jitiya)?
Observed primarily by mothers across Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Nepal, and Jharkhand, Jivitputrika is an unyielding, waterless (*nirjala*) 24-to-36 hour penance dedicated to Lord Jimutavahana for the protection, health, and prosperity of children.

### What Happens at Lakshmi Kund on Day 16:
- **03:00 AM – 06:30 AM (Pre-Dawn Rush):** The busiest period of the entire festival. Tens of thousands of mothers arrive on foot from across the city, dressed in auspicious red and yellow sarees.
- **The Sacred Bamboo Basket (*Pauti*):** Devotees carry a round bamboo basket painted with vermilion (*sindoor*). Inside the basket rests the traditional festive prasad:
  - Freshly fried **Thekua** (hard jaggery-wheat cakes) and **Pirukiya** (Gujiya stuffed with roasted mawa or semolina).
  - **Satapuri** (crispy layered festive sweet).
  - Soaked and sprouted **Kala Chana** (black gram).
  - Wild miniature cucumbers (**Chhoti Kheri**), sweet potatoes, and custard apples.
  - Clay figurines representing *Chilhoro* (the eagle) and *Siyaro* (the jackal), the two mythical witnesses of the Jitiya Vrat.
- **The Ritual Immersion:** After completing prayers before the Trinity and Sorahia Mahadev, devotees submerge themselves in Lakshmi Kund, untie the 16-knot thread, offer flowers and a portion of the prasad to the waters, and take the remaining energized prasad home for their children.

## Last-Mile Transit: How to Reach Lakshmi Kund Through Traffic Barricades

The biggest headache for visiting pilgrims is assuming a taxi or auto can drive directly to the temple steps. 

### Traffic Reality on Luxa Road
During the 16-day fair, the Varanasi Traffic Police issues strict traffic diversions:
- **Luxa Road (between Girjaghar and Gurubagh) is closed to all four-wheelers and commercial tempos** from 04:00 AM to 11:00 AM and 04:00 PM to 10:00 PM.
- On Day 16 (Jitiya), Luxa Road is placed under **100% Pedestrian-Only Enforcement**. Even e-rickshaws are halted at the outer crossings.

### Strategic Drop Points for Cabs & Private Cars

1. **West Gate: Gurubagh Crossing (Recommended):**
   - Approached smoothly from Sigra, Rathyatra, or Cantonment Railway Station without entering the congested old city.
   - Drop your vehicle at Gurubagh Crossing. Walk eastward along Luxa Road for **450 metres (approx. 6–8 minutes)** directly into Lakshmi Kund.
   - Flat walking surface; plenty of shops along the way.
2. **East Gate: Girjaghar Crossing (Godowlia Side):**
   - If staying near Dashashwamedh or Assi, cars drop at Girjaghar Crossing (near St. Thomas Church).
   - Walk westward along Luxa Road for **550 metres (approx. 8–10 minutes)** to reach the kund.
3. **South Gate: Kamachha Crossing:**
   - E-rickshaws can take you from Kamachha through Ramapura lanes, dropping within 250 metres of the southern embankment.

## Senior Citizens & Safety Protocols at the Kund

If you are accompanying elderly parents or grandparents, keep these vital local ground facts in mind:

1. **Slippery Stone Steps:**
   - Over thousands of morning dips, wet water, spilled milk, oil, and wet vermilion create a slick film over the stone ghat steps.
   - **Advice:** Ensure seniors wear rubber-soled footwear with grip. Avoid leather slippers. Walk along the designated dry central pathways where jute mats are laid out by the municipal committee.
2. **The "Marjan" Option for Frail Pilgrims:**
   - Scriptural rules state that for elderly or ailing devotees, stepping down into deep water is not mandatory. Performing **Marjan**—sitting safely on the top stone steps, cleansing feet and hands, and having sacred kund water sprinkled over the head—carries equal spiritual merit.
3. **Dedicated Women's Bathing Enclosure:**
   - A section of the northern ghat is cordoned off with metal barricades and tarpaulin curtains as a private changing area for women.
4. **Avoid the Peak Day-16 Crush:**
   - On Day 16 (Jitiya), the rush between 04:00 AM and 07:00 AM is overwhelming. If traveling with elderly family members, arrive either between **08:30 AM and 10:30 AM** or on Days 14–15 when the crowds are manageable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can men take bath in Lakshmi Kund during Sorahia Mela?
Yes. While the majority of devotees are women observing the Mahalakshmi and Jivitputrika fasts, men and young boys also take holy dips in the kund and offer prayers to Sorahia Mahadev. Designated separate bathing areas are demarcated for men and women.

### Is photography permitted around Lakshmi Kund?
Photography of the outdoor stone steps and street fair is permitted. However, photography inside the sanctum sanctorum of the Mahalakshmi Temple is strictly prohibited. Always respect the privacy of women bathing along the ghat steps.

### Where can we find sattvic fasting (phalahari) food near Luxa?
Reputed sweet and snack houses along the Sigra–Rathyatra road (such as Ksheer Sagar and Ram Bhandar outlets) serve hygienic phalahari items: singhara puri, sabudana khichdi, makhana kheer, and fresh fruit bowls prepared strictly with rock salt (*sendha namak*).
