# Chetganj Nakkataiya Mela 2026 Varanasi: Dates, Night Route, Tableaux & Cab Guide

> **Quick 2026 Date Check:** Chetganj Nakkataiya happens on the night of **Karwa Chauth (Thursday, 29 October 2026)** and runs straight through to the morning of **Friday, 30 October 2026**. The stage leela starts around **10:30 PM**, and the big procession of **Laag-Vimaan floats and moving tableaux (*Chaukiyan*)** starts rolling around **midnight (12:00 AM)** and doesn't stop until sunrise (**06:00 AM**).

Ask any tourist about Banaras and they'll tell you about morning boat rides at Assi Ghat or watching the evening Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh. But ask anyone who grew up around Chetganj, Lahurabir, or Nai Sarak, and they'll grin and ask you: *"Bhaiyya, kabhi Nakkataiya dekhe ho?"* (Brother, have you ever seen Nakkataiya?)

Because on Karwa Chauth night, half of Varanasi simply doesn't go to sleep. Literally. Between 10 at night and 6 the next morning, anywhere between **4 to 5 lakh people** pack into a 2.5-kilometer stretch of road from Maldahiya to Chetganj. It’s what locals call a true *Lakkha Mela*—a fair where the crowd counts in lakhs. 

You've got 40-foot moving floats, brass bands having loud musical duels, wrestling akharas spinning fiery torches (*banethi*), and kids dressed as Kali and Shiva hanging from hidden iron rods 20 feet up in the air looking like they're literally flying.

Every year, I see first-time visitors make the same three mistakes:
1. **They show up at 3:00 PM** thinking it’s a normal street fair, see closed shutters and barricades, and wonder if they got the date wrong.
2. **They try booking an Uber or auto to Chetganj at 9:00 PM**, get dumped at a police checkpoint 2 km away, and have no way to get back to their hotel at 2:00 AM.
3. **They book a hotel room right inside Chetganj or Nai Sarak**, only to discover that cars are banned from 6:00 PM onwards, forcing them to drag their heavy trolley bags on foot through a sea of people.

I wrote this guide to save you the headache: the actual schedule, the clever anti-British history behind it, where police will actually stop your car, where to stand so you don't get squished, and where to grab piping hot kachoris at 4:30 in the morning.

## The Secret History: How a Banarasi Baba Mocked the British in 1887

Most people think Nakkataiya is just a religious Ramlila procession. It isn't. It actually started as a daring act of **political protest against the British Empire**.

Back in **1887**, thirty years after the 1857 revolt, the British Raj had clamped down hard on India. You couldn't hold political rallies, you couldn't give patriotic speeches, and you couldn't gather in public without colonial spies and police arresting you for sedition.

A sharp Banarasi saint and cultural leader named **Baba Fateh Ram** spotted a loophole: the British were terrified of touching Hindu religious processions because they didn't want another mutiny on their hands.

So Baba Fateh Ram took the Ramayana story of **Surpanakha's Nakkataiya (the cutting off of her nose)** and turned it into an open political joke:
> *Cutting off the nose of the arrogant demoness Surpanakha was a public taunt for cutting off the haughty, imperial "nose" of the British Raj.*

### Freedom Fighters Hiding in the Noise
The fair quickly became the ultimate underground meetup:
- With hundreds of thousands of people shouting, drums banging, and police completely overwhelmed trying to manage traffic, revolutionaries like **Chandrashekhar Azad** and **Sachindra Nath Sanyal** would slip into the crowd wearing common Banarasi dhotis and kurtas.
- They would pass secret messages, plan safehouses, hand out banned freedom leaflets, and melt back into the alleyways without the police having a clue who was who.
- That rebellious spirit never left. Even today, alongside statues of Shiva and Durga, you'll see floats showing Indian soldiers at the border, ISRO rockets, and hilarious satirical skits mocking corrupt politicians and inflation.

## What on Earth is a "Laag-Vimaan"?

If you've never seen one, it's hard to describe. 

A **Vimaan** is basically a massive, multi-tiered decorated stage built on the back of a truck or a tractor-trailer. But the **Laag** (लाग) is what makes everyone stop and stare.

```
       [ Child Actor dressed as Kali / Shiva ]
                          |
             [ Padded Chest & Waist Harness ]
                          |
             [ Hidden Spring-Steel Rod ("Kamani") ]  <--- Wrapped in clouds & silk
                          |
               [ Heavy Steel Counterweight ]
                          |
       =================================================
       [ TRUCK BED WITH 10,000 BLINKING LED LIGHTS ]
       =================================================
```

Local blacksmiths and metal artisans from Maldahiya, Chetganj, and Lallapura take spring-steel rods—the same kind used in truck shock absorbers, called *kamani*—and bend them into strong cantilever frames. 

Young actors (usually agile local kids dressed as Kali, Shiva, Durga, or Hanuman) are strapped into padded harnesses attached to these rods. The metal frame is then cleverly wrapped in velvet cloth, artificial cotton clouds, or lotus garlands so you can't see the steel from below.

Standing on the road, looking up in the dark with the lights shining, **it looks like a real child is hovering in thin air, 15 to 20 feet up**, striking divine poses with swords or tridents. It takes serious guts and incredible balance.

### Other Things You'll See Along the Route:
- **Akharas & Banethi:** Traditional wrestlers (*pehlwans*) from local Banaras wrestling gyms lead the floats. Young men spin *banethi*—ropes soaked in kerosene and set on fire—spinning them in fast, roaring circles of flame in the middle of the street.
- **Brass Band Duels:** Two brass bands on adjacent floats will literally stop, face each other, and try to blast their trumpets and drums louder than the other to win the crowd's cheers.

## Taking Elderly Parents or Kids? Read This First

If you're bringing older family members or young children, you need a realistic game plan. You cannot just stroll into the crowd at 1:00 AM with a stroller or a wheelchair—it simply will not work.

### Two Dangerous Spots to Avoid
- ❌ **Do not stand near Chetganj Police Station or the sharp bend at Chaur Chhatwa between 12:30 AM and 3:30 AM.** The road narrows down here, and every time a 40-foot tractor float tries to turn the corner, the crowd surges forward. It gets uncomfortably tight.
- ❌ **Don't wear slippers or flip-flops.** Wear sturdy sneakers. In a crowd of 4 lakh people, if someone accidentally steps on the heel of your slipper, you'll lose it in two seconds and spend the rest of the night walking barefoot on pavement.

### How Smart Families Do It:
1. **The Early Viewing Trick (9:30 PM to 10:30 PM at Patel Chowk):**
   This is what locals with elderly parents do. All the floats gather near Patel Chowk in Maldahiya before the parade begins. The lights are already on, the generators are humming, but the massive midnight crowd hasn't arrived yet. You can walk around in relative peace, inspect the craftsmanship, click great photos with the performers, and head back to your hotel before the madness starts.
2. **Book a Balcony Spot:**
   Along Chetganj Main Road, many local Banarasi families and small guesthouses let travelers sit on their first-floor balconies or rooftops for a small fee. You get an unobstructed view of every float, martial arts display, and band without a single person bumping into you.
3. **Keep an Emergency Spot:**
   Cellular networks usually get choked around 1:00 AM because thousands of people are uploading videos at once. If your phone stops connecting, you can't call each other. Agree beforehand on an obvious meetup spot—like the Sardar Patel statue at Maldahiya.

## The 4:00 AM Food Trail: What to Eat While Staying Up All Night

Nakkataiya night is basically a giant street-food marathon. Street carts and sweet shops keep their stoves blazing until dawn:

1. **Midnight Tamatar Chaat:**
   Piping hot, spicy, tangy tomato mash cooked in pure desi ghee, spiced with roasted cumin, topped with cashews and namkeen, and served in an earthen *kulhad*. Head to **Deena Chaat Bhandar** (Luxa Road) or the street stalls near Lahurabir.
2. **The Season's First Malaiyo:**
   Late October is when **Malaiyo** (मलइयो) officially makes its winter debut in Varanasi. Milk is left outside overnight to catch the autumn dew, then churned into a light-as-air saffron foam, served in earthen cups with pistachios and almonds. On Nakkataiya night, the very first pots of the season are sold around 2:00 AM.
3. **04:30 AM Hot Kachori-Jalebi:**
   When the last float rolls into Benia Tiraha at dawn, follow the crowd into **Ram Bhandar** in Thatheri Bazar, **Shree Rajbandhu** in Kachori Gali, or the heritage halwais near Lahurabir. Grab freshly fried *chhoti kachoris* with spicy potato-chana curry and hot, crispy *desi ghee jalebis*.
4. **Cold Thandai & Maghai Paan:**
   Cap off the sunrise with a brass glass of chilled saffron-badam thandai near Godowlia, followed by a sweet, tender *Maghai Meetha Paan* that literally melts in your mouth without chewing.

## Common Questions Visitors Ask

### Does it cost anything to watch the mela?
No, it is a completely free public street festival. You don't need any tickets or passes to stand on the street and watch the parade. You only pay if you decide to rent a private balcony or terrace spot from a local family along the main road.

### What if it rains?
Late October in Banaras is dry, clear autumn weather with nighttime temperatures around 20°C (68°F). It virtually never rains. Even if a rare drizzle happens, the floats carry heavy waterproof tarpaulins to protect the generator systems and sound consoles.

### Can I take photos and videos?
Absolutely. The performers love the camera, and people take thousands of photos. Pro tip: stand near the big Chandannagar arch lights at Patel Chowk or Lahurabir Crossing if you want clean lighting without harsh phone flashes.

### How is Nakkataiya related to Bharat Milap?
Both are famous *Lakkha Melas* in Varanasi's autumn calendar. [Bharat Milap at Nati Imli](/en/bharat-milap-nati-imli) happens the afternoon right after Dussehra (21 October 2026), celebrating the reunion of Rama and Bharat. Chetganj Nakkataiya happens eight days later on Karwa Chauth night (29 October 2026), celebrating the defeat of Surpanakha and Banaras's historic defiance against the British.
