## Etymology and history: why three names survived

### Kashi — the ancient "city of light"

**Kashi** is the oldest-feeling name and comes from Sanskrit traditions around light, radiance and spiritual illumination. It is the name of devotion: Kashi Vishwanath, Kashi khand, moksha, darshan, puja, aarti and the belief that Shiva's city is not just a destination but a liberation field.

Use this name when your trip is built around **Kashi Vishwanath darshan**, temple timing, family elders, puja or a vow.

### Varanasi — the official name from Varuna + Assi

**Varanasi** is the modern official name used on airport boards, train tickets, hotel addresses, government documents and maps. The usual explanation is geographic: the city lies between the **Varuna** river in the north and the **Assi** area/stream in the south.

Use this name when you are solving logistics: airport transfer, station pickup, hotel area, route sequence, parking, itinerary and reliable transport.

### Banaras / Benares — the cultural and affectionate name

**Banaras** is the name that carries the city's everyday soul. It became widely used through medieval/Mughal-era speech and later appeared in colonial English as **Benares**. Locals still use Banaras affectionately for the food, music, paan, silk, lanes, humour and unpolished charm that do not fit neatly into a temple timetable.

Use this name when your trip is about tasting, wandering, listening, shopping and feeling the city rather than only checking sights.

## FAQ

> **What is the difference between Kashi, Varanasi and Banaras?**
> They are three names for the same city, but each name carries a different mood. Kashi is the ancient sacred name linked to pilgrimage and light, Varanasi is the official administrative name, and Banaras or Benares is the affectionate cultural name locals still use for food, music and everyday life.

> **Are Kashi and Varanasi the same place?**
> Yes. Kashi and Varanasi are not separate towns or different temple areas; they refer to the same city on the Ganga. Travellers usually say Kashi when they are thinking about Kashi Vishwanath, darshan, puja and the spiritual side of the trip.

> **Why is Varanasi called Banaras or Benares?**
> Banaras is the older popular name that grew through medieval and Mughal-era usage, while Benares became the familiar colonial English spelling. It remains a warm local word, especially when people talk about Banarasi food, silk, music, paan and the city's cultural soul.

> **Why is Varanasi called Kashi?**
> Kashi is an ancient Sanskrit name often explained as the "city of light" or the shining city. It is the name most strongly connected with Shiva, Kashi Vishwanath and the belief that the city grants liberation, so pilgrims naturally use it more than casual visitors do.

> **Which name should I use when planning my trip?**
> Use the name that matches your intent. If your priority is darshan, think Kashi and plan temple timing first. If your priority is food, music and lanes, think Banaras and plan a culture day. If your priority is airport, hotel and official logistics, think Varanasi.

> **Is Banaras the official name of the city?**
> No. The official name is Varanasi, commonly explained from the Varuna and Assi rivers. Banaras is still widely understood and loved, but you will usually see Varanasi on airport boards, railway tickets, hotel addresses, government forms and maps.
