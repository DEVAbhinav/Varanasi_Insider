Sample Itinerary – Ayodhya in One Day
We’ve crafted an optimized itinerary covering Ayodhya’s must-see spiritual sites while keeping the
journey comfortable:

Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Temple: Visit the birthplace of Lord Rama. Ram Lala Temple (within
the Janmabhoomi complex) is the highlight – it’s the temple under construction but open for
darshan . Spend time in prayer and soak in the spiritual vibes of this iconic site.
Hanuman Garhi: Next, visit Hanuman Garhi Temple, a short drive from Ram Janmabhoomi.
This hilltop temple of Lord Hanuman requires a climb of ~70 steps. The panoramic view of
Ayodhya from the top is worth the effort.
Kanak Bhavan & Sita Rasoi: Explore Kanak Bhavan, a palace-like temple devoted to Sita and
Rama, known for its gold-plated spires. Nearby is Sita Rasoi, a small temple resembling the
legendary kitchen of Goddess Sita . These sites give a glimpse into Ayodhya’s heritage
beyond the main temple.
Lunch Break (1:00–2:00 PM): Enjoy a traditional vegetarian lunch at a recommended local
eatery (we can suggest clean, group-friendly restaurants). Relish local specialties like kachorisabzi if you’re adventurous!
Dasrath Mahal: After lunch, if time permits, visit King Dashrath’s Palace, an old temple
representing Rama’s father’s royal residence. It’s a quick stop but adds context to the Ramayana
history of Ayodhya .
Afternoon: Optional Saryu Aarti: If you’re ahead of schedule and interested, we can organize a
brief participation in the Saryu Aarti (evening prayer at the river) around 4 PM. Otherwise, we
begin the journey back.
Depart Ayodhya (~4:30 PM): Settle back into the Tempo Traveller for the return ride. We usually
take a short tea break mid-way.
Return to Varanasi (~9:00 PM): You’ll arrive back in Varanasi by late evening (approx. 9 PM,
depending on traffic). Tired but spiritually fulfilled!
This itinerary is flexible. If your group wants to spend more time at a particular place (for example,
extended prayer at Ram Janmabhoomi or shopping for prasad and souvenirs in Ayodhya’s market), just
let us know. We buffer in some extra time since it’s a private tour for your group only.

Why Prayagraj?
Prayagraj (formerly Allahabad) is one of the holiest cities in India, forming an essential part of the
Kashi–Prayag spiritual circuit . It is home to the Triveni Sangam, the confluence of three rivers –
Ganga, Yamuna, and the mystical Saraswati. A bath at the Sangam is believed to wash away all sins.
Prayagraj also hosts the grand Kumbh Mela. For those in Varanasi, a day trip to Prayagraj is both
spiritually rewarding and logistically feasible, thanks to a modern highway connecting the two cities .
Distance, Route & Travel Time
Distance: ~121 km by road from Varanasi to Prayagraj (Sangam area) .
Travel Time: ~2.5 to 3 hours one way, via the NH19 (Varanasi–Handia–Prayagraj) expressway

start by 7:00 AM from Varanasi, reaching Prayagraj around 10:00 AM.
The Route: We take the NH19, passing towns like Bhadohi and Handia. This is a smooth route
with plenty of dhabas/petrol pumps. Our drivers are familiar with the best pit stops – whether
you need a morning chai break or clean washrooms, we know where to halt .

The Prayagraj Darshan is no ordinary sightseeing; it’s a deeply spiritual journey. Pilgrims often say

Kumbh Mela 2025 will be at Prayagraj – if you plan to visit during the Mela, let us know early for special
arrangements (crowds are massive, but we have experience navigating pilgrims during Kumbh).

FAQ – Important Questions
Q: Can we do Varanasi–Prayagraj and back in one day comfortably?
A: Yes, absolutely. It’s ~3 hours each way. We’ve designed the itinerary to cover the essentials without
exhaustion. Many pilgrims visit Prayagraj from Varanasi as a day trip and find it quite comfortable in our
Tempo Travellers.
Q: How much time can we spend at Triveni Sangam?
A: As much as you need. Typically, people spend about 1 to 1.5 hours (boat ride, bathing, puja). If you
plan a special ritual that takes longer, just let us know and we’ll adjust other parts of the itinerary. We
keep buffer time in the schedule for Sangam because it’s the main purpose of the trip.
Q: Are there life jackets for the boat ride?
A: Yes, the boat operators at Sangam usually have life jackets. We partner with trusted boatmen for our
customers. Safety is a priority. If your group has elderly or non-swimmers, they can still go on the boat
and perform the rituals while seated in the boat – dipping hands in the water, etc., which many do.
Q: What about changing rooms after the dip?
A: There are makeshift arrangements at Sangam. Alternatively, some choose to change in the Tempo
Traveller itself (windows have curtains). We can also arrange a private tent for ladies to change by the
Sangam shore for a small fee. Just let the driver know in advance.
Q: Is Anand Bhavan worth it?
A: It’s entirely up to your interest. It’s a museum (entry ~₹20) showing Nehru family’s memorabilia. Not
a pilgrimage site, but some enjoy the historical aspect as a bonus. If you’re tight on time or not keen,
you can replace it with more time at another temple like Sri Mankameshwar (a Shiva temple by the
Yamuna) or simply start back early. Our itinerary is your itinerary – you have the freedom to customize.

Key Sights Covered
Our local sightseeing route is customizable, but here’s a popular circuit that covers the essence of
“Kashi Darshan”:
Shri Kashi Vishwanath Temple: Start at the most revered temple of Varanasi, dedicated to Lord
Shiva. Morning is a good time to visit to avoid huge crowds (except on special festival days). Our
driver will drop you as close as possible to the temple (Godowlia Chowk), from where it’s a short

Durga Kund Temple: Also known as the Red Temple, dedicated to Goddess Durga. It’s near BHU.

monkeys though – our driver will mind the vehicle, and you should mind your prasad!
Tulsi Manas Mandir: Walking distance from Durga temple, this serene temple has the entire
Ramcharitmanas inscribed on its walls. It’s a tribute to Goswami Tulsidas. We often include it if
time permits, as it’s on the same circuit in the Lanka area.

Significance of Bodhgaya & Gaya
Bodhgaya: It’s the place where Prince Siddhartha meditated under the Bodhi Tree and attained
enlightenment to become the Buddha. The centerpiece is the Mahabodhi Temple, with the
descendant of that original Bodhi Tree still there . It’s the most sacred site in Buddhism.
Around it have flourished dozens of monasteries and temples built by various countries
(Thailand, Bhutan, Japan, etc all have their monasteries there), so it’s like a mini United Nations
of Buddhism – very serene and culturally rich. Visiting Bodhgaya is a moving experience for
many – you see monks in maroon or saffron robes from all over the world, chanting, meditating.
The Mahabodhi Temple complex has a calm energy; even as a non-Buddhist one feels the peace.
Gaya (Vishnupad Temple): In Hindu tradition, Gaya is one of the holiest places to perform Pind
Daan – rituals for departed souls. The Vishnupad Temple supposedly has Lord Vishnu’s footprint
and is the epicenter for these rites. Many Hindus who come to Varanasi also go to Gaya,
believing that performing rites at both places ensures moksha (liberation) for their ancestors
. The ritual usually involves pundits guiding you with offerings by the Phalgu River. It’s an
ancient tradition (even in the epic Ramayana, Lord Rama is said to have come to Gaya for his
father’s pind daan). So, spiritually, combining Kashi and Gaya is seen as very fulfilling.
Other sites in Bodhgaya: The Great Buddha Statue (80-ft tall) is iconic – often visited for a
quick photo op and prayer. Sujata village across the river (where a lady Sujata gave milk-rice to
the starving Siddhartha; there’s a stupa). Dungeshwari Cave (Mahakala caves) on the way
where Siddhartha meditated before Bodhgaya – some like to stop here too if following Buddha’s
path.
Nalanda/Rajgir (optional): Some groups extend the trip by a day to cover Nalanda University
ruins and Rajgir (where Buddha preached). If you have time, we can take you further ~70 km
from Bodhgaya to these sites. But that becomes a multi-day Buddhist circuit tour. Our Tempo
Traveller and driver can definitely do it if you plan accordingly.

Pilgrimage & Yatra Tours by Tempo Traveller
Varanasi is often the starting point for numerous pilgrimages (yatras) in North India. From Shakti

Why Tempo Traveller for Yatras?
Pilgrimages often involve traveling with family or fellow devotees. A Tempo Traveller allows the entire
group (say 10-15 people) to stay together, pray together en route, and maintain a spiritual atmosphere
throughout the journey. For instance, you can play bhajans on board, have collective chanting, or simply
discuss legends of the places you’ll visit – all in one vehicle. It fosters a communal spiritual vibe.
Additionally, carrying pooja samagri (ritual items), luggage, even sometimes cooking utensils for prasad
(some groups carry), is easier with the ample space . Our drivers are sensitive to pilgrimage needs –
like identifying clean dhabas for only vegetarian food if you observe that, or timing journeys to reach
before aarti times, etc.
